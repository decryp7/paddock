## 1. Measured: where the time goes

### 1.1 The runners

| Class | Machine (job logs, `build-jobs: this host`) | Concurrency | Share of job-minutes (16 full train/main runs) | Queue wait of a ready job, p50 / p90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Linux | one host, `d-gtr9`: 16 cores, 128 GB, jobs in the `paddock-ci` container | max 4 seen, but 3 jobs for 64% of its busy time and 4 for under 1% | **2,003 min, 63%** | **13.5 / 42.8 min** (max 72) |
| Windows | `decryp7ology-dev`: Proxmox VM, 16 vCPU, 32 GB | 3 | 696 min, 22% | 0.1 / 10.2 min |
| macOS | `d7-mac-mini`, `-2`, `-3`: three runners on the developer Mac (M4 Pro, 14 cores, 64 GB), shared with the agents | 3 x 1 | 492 min, 15% | 3.8 / 11.9 min |

Over all workflows and the last 20 runs each, `./dev ci timings` gives the same picture: `d-gtr9` 8,867 busy minutes, Windows
2,779, the three Macs 2,613 together. **The user is right: Linux carries almost two thirds of CI, and it is the only class
with a real queue.** "Queue" here is a job's start minus the end of the jobs its `needs` name (the plan job for most), so it
is pure waiting for a runner.

### 1.2 One train round: two workflows, one after the other

A train's round is `ci.yml` then `debrief.yml`, dispatched one after the other: run 8328 was created at 01:23:00, 15 s after
8327 ended; 8230/8238, 8283/8297 show the same. The two main-push runs of a landing start together (8315/8316).

| Run | Wall | Critical path without queueing | Linux job-min | Mac | Windows | Last job |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8327 `ci.yml`, train 15 | 56.2 min | 30.3 | 133 | 49 | 59 | `paddock-dev (ubuntu)` after a 51-min queue |
| 8328 `debrief.yml`, train 15 | 57.0 | 35.1 | 93 | 18 | 33 | `end-to-end (shard 4/4)` after a 33-min queue |
| 8315 / 8316, main push of train 14, together | 78.0 / 89.9 | 29.3 / 38.4 | 129 / 113 | | | Linux jobs queued 71 and 70 min |
| Full `ci.yml` runs, 2026-11-15 to 11-20 (23) | 41 to 78 | 27 to 31 | 117 to 158 | | | |
| Full `debrief.yml` runs (11) | 35 to 91 | 30 to 41 | 86 to 116 | | | |

So a green round costs about **95 to 115 minutes of wall time today**, of which the work on the critical path is about 30
plus 35. **The difference is the Linux queue**: about 226 Linux job-minutes per round on 3 lanes is a 75-minute floor.

### 1.3 What the Linux minutes are

Phases from the logs of all 63 jobs of 8327 and 8328 (`Finished ... in`, Playwright's `N passed (T)`, nextest's `Summary`):

| Class | Playwright | cargo compile | nextest run | Rest (setup, npm, Angular, checks) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Linux (226 job-min) | **60%** | 12% | 3% | 25% |
| macOS (67) | 19% | 34% | 22% | 25% |
| Windows (92) | 0% | 48% | 15% | 37% |

The Linux jobs are browser tests, not builds. The longest ones (median over 11 full runs):

| Job | Median | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `app dyno (ubuntu, e2e-webkit)` | **27.7 min** | 448 WebKit tests on **one worker**, no shard; `ci.yml`'s critical path in 8327 and 8283 |
| `debrief end-to-end` shards 3 and 4 | 22.6 / 24.2 | 355 tests each on 2 workers; shards 1 and 2 take 11.8 / 12.9. Same count, double time: shards 3 and 4 hold the WebKit project (`live-tail-follow` tests at 10 to 22 s each) |
| `app dyno (ubuntu, e2e-1/2)` | 12.2 / 10.7 | 224 Chromium tests per shard, 1 worker |
| `reference apps (shard 1/2)` | 10.6 | **521** tests against 256 in shard 2 (file-level sharding), 1 worker |
| `app pitwall (ubuntu, e2e-webkit)` | 7.7 | 245 tests, 1 worker |

Every Linux Playwright job but Debrief's runs `--workers=1` (`apps/dyno/paddock.ci.json` lines 62 and 70,
`apps/dyno/app/playwright.config.ts` `workers: 1`). Train runs are dispatched with `webkit: true` (8327's plan log), so the
WebKit legs are part of every train round.

**Build once, fan out?** Each Linux e2e job spends 33 to 51 s (mean 48 s over 32 jobs) before its first test: install,
`npm run build -w ui`, the app build. Sharing that build saves at most about 30 s x 15 jobs, **about 8 job-min a round (3%)**.
The Rust compile is 12% of Linux and spread over separate Cargo workspaces (each app, `crates/`, `tools/paddock-dev`), which
cannot share a target folder; inside one workspace the jobs use different profiles (clippy, test, `dist`). Build-once
fan-out is common practice (nextest archives; GitHub's upload/download-artifact pattern) but is **not** the lever here.

### 1.4 The other critical-path items

- **`./dev` is built from source in train runs.** It is published for `main` only (ADR 0022 amendment, "main-only publish
  bot"), so 14 of the 63 jobs of 8327/8328 built it: 1,133 s in all, 28 to 44 s on Linux, 32 to 72 s on the Mac, **69 to 272 s
  on Windows** (`crate (windows-latest)` 272 s, `app dyno (windows-latest)` 160 s then 129 s more). On Windows the `build-jobs`
  step, where the build happens, is 69 of 696 job-min (10%).
- **Debrief's Windows bundle** (26.5 min median) builds twice with the `dist` profile (`lto = "thin"`, `codegen-units = 1`,
  `apps/debrief/Cargo.toml` `[profile.dist]`): the sidecars (`cargo build -p debrief-plugins -p debrief-store-worker`,
  3 min 58 s), then the app through `tauri build` (12 min 15 s), one after the other on `CARGO_BUILD_JOBS=5`.
- **Setup** is 7% of Linux job time in step terms (checkout 54 min over 16 runs), 14% on macOS and 22% on Windows (the `./dev`
  build above, checkout, `setup-rust`).

### 1.5 Red rounds are the biggest cost

Of 34 full train and main runs from 2026-11-15 to 11-20, **6 were green**. The failing jobs, by count: `paddock-dev
(windows)` 9, `reference apps (shard 1/2)` 7, `crate (windows)` 6, `crates` (fmt, hack, deny) 4, WebKit on macOS 4, then
Pitwall and Dyno WebKit, Debrief shards 3 and 4, `tools` and `paddock-dev (ubuntu)` 3 each. Train 12 took from 8138
(2026-11-15 20:00) to its main push 8198 (11-17 07:14); train 13 from 8208 (11-17 14:04) to 8241 (11-18 09:36); train 14
from 8245 (11-18 10:53) to 8315 (11-19 21:34). **Trains land in 20 to 35 hours, not in one 100-minute round.** One run more
or less per round matters less than a round that is green the first time.

Two process facts from the same runs:

- Train 14 landed `9cbd77a63` after fix rounds only: its last `ci.yml` runs on the train were partial (8296 on `411813e23`,
  13 Linux job-min) and its `debrief.yml` 8307 ran 2 jobs. The first full run of the landed tree was the main push 8315,
  and it was red (fixed by `fix/main-red-8315` on train 15). Rule 50 (b) and ER-10 already ask for the full green run on
  the final tree before landing; the main push run then found what that run would have.
- When the final tree did have its full green run, the main push run repeats it on the same commit: about 240 Linux
  job-minutes that hold the lanes the next train needs.

## 4. Recommendation, ranked

Ranking weighs the user's point first: Linux is 63% of the minutes and the only queue, and most of it is Playwright.

**Tier 1, quick wins (about 3 agent-days, no host change).**

1. Run a train's two workflows together (item 2): 0.5 day, about 20 min a round now and the full benefit once lanes grow.
2. Balance and split the Playwright shards (item 3): 1 day, `ci.yml` critical path 30 to about 17 min, Debrief 24 to 17.
3. Linux e2e not behind stage 1 in the staged CI (item 6): 0.25 day inside the in-flight branch.
4. `./dev` without a source build on train runs (item 5, the host-cache form): 0.5 to 1 day.
5. Skip the duplicate main push run (item 7): 0.5 day. It frees about 240 Linux job-min per landing.
6. Lockfile checks off the heavy queue (item 8): 0.25 day.

Projected green round after tier 1: **about 75 min** (est.: the two workflows together on 3 lanes, about 226 Linux job-min,
so the Linux floor of 75 min still decides), from about 100 to 115 today.

**Tier 2, the Linux lever (about 4 agent-days plus the user's host change).**

7. Linux capacity 3 to 6 (item 1), after a sampler run shows CPU and memory headroom on `d-gtr9`; or a second Linux runner
   (1b) if it does not. 0.5 day plus the host change.
8. Playwright workers 3 on Linux (item 4, ADR 0080 WP-4): 3 days, with the 20-run isolation check per engine.

Projected green round after tier 2: **about 40 to 45 min** (est.: Linux job-min about 150, a floor of 25 min on 6 lanes, so
the critical path decides: Debrief's Windows bundle 26.5 + smoke 9.9 + plan, about 38).

**Tier 3, bigger changes (about 3 agent-days).**

9. Debrief Windows bundle in one cargo invocation (item 9): 0.5 day, the critical path to about 33 to 35 min. Codegen
   units on trains only with the user's yes.
10. Test-level selection on branch and fix rounds (item 10): 2 days; not on the train run.
11. nextest partitions for Windows `paddock-dev` (item 11): 1 day, only if it stays on a critical path after tier 2.

Not recommended: Bazel or Buck2 (item 13), and build-once fan-out for Linux (item 12) until the rest is done: both cost more
than they save here.

**The largest saving is not a tier: first-time-green rounds.** 6 of 34 full runs were green, and trains took 20 to 35 hours.
The in-flight member checks on the real runners and the staged CI attack this; after them, the Windows tests that fail most
(`paddock-dev`, `crate`) are worth a triage pass with `./dev ci triage` and `./dev ci flakes`, under ER-12 and ER-28.

### Landing wall time, today and per tier

A landing is: member gates (local), one green train round, the landing itself. Red rounds come on top.

| | Local gate per member (p50 / p90) | One green round | Landing with one red round first |
|---|---|---|---|
| Today (measured) | 5 / 40 min (72% queue) | 95 to 115 min, serial workflows | about 3.5 to 4 h plus the fix |
| After tier 1 (est.) | 3 / 30 min (lockfile checks off the queue) | about 75 min | about 2 h with stage 1 stopping the red round early |
| After tier 2 (est.) | the same | about 40 to 45 min | about 1 h 15 min |
| After tier 3 (est.) | the same | about 33 to 35 min | about 1 h |

The local gate stays queue-bound while the Mac runs at load 40 to 58: the member checks on the real runners move the
heavy Linux and Windows work off the Mac, which is the change that helps here; nothing in this note adds more heavy work to it.

### The cheaper common-practice option, for each item beyond it

- Item 1 against 1b: raising `capacity` is the common-practice step; a second host is only for when the sampler shows
  `d-gtr9` has no headroom.
- Item 4 (3 days) against item 3 (1 day): sharding alone is the cheaper, common option and gives most of the critical-path
  gain; workers mainly cut job-minutes, which matters only while lanes stay at 3.
- Item 9's codegen units: the cheaper option is the release profile as today (no decision needed).
- Item 10: the cheaper option is ADR 0080's accepted package-level selection without Playwright `--only-changed`.

### Risks

- At 6 lanes, timing-sensitive tests slow down on a shared host (ER-12). Pass condition: the sampler run, then 20 full
  Linux runs at the new capacity with the flake rate compared with today's.
- Sharding WebKit legs shows order-dependent specs that a single worker hid. That is a finding to fix, not a reason to
  stop (ER-29).
- Skipping the main push run relies on landing only after a full green run on the exact commit; the verdict reader must
  refuse partial runs (`./dev ci verdict` counts only `plan mode: full=true` runs).

## 5. Decisions for the user

- **D1. Linux capacity:** raise `d-gtr9` to 6 jobs after the sampler run (recommended), or add a second Linux runner on
  other hardware, or keep 3. It is a change to your runner config.
- **D2. Train rounds:** run `ci.yml` and `debrief.yml` of one train together (recommended), or keep them one after the other.
- **D3. `./dev` on trains:** a host-wide build per inputs hash (recommended; keeps the main-only publish bot), or publish for
  `train/*` too.
- **D4. Main push run:** skip it when the commit has a full green train run (recommended, as merge trains and bors do), or
  keep it.
- **D5. Debrief bundle on trains:** keep the release profile (cheaper, no change), or `codegen-units = 16` on train rounds only.
